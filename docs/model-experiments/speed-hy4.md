# Hy4-flash performance on AngelSlim Q3_K

Stark's best measured prefill and decode use different load profiles. A streamed worker with 4,096-row prefill chunks and guarded FFN device views reached **711.61 tok/s first-use prefill** on the fixed 4K fixture. A prepared resident worker reached **58.28 tok/s warm K2 decode** on that fixture. An exploratory resident K3 run peaked at **68.58 tok/s** on one short TypeScript task. These are worker diagnostics with their own build and workload controls. The standard benchmark profile is still draft. ## Prefill

**Batch the packed expert route first.** The early native worker matched all 64 reference outputs at 4K with the original projector, both with drafting off and with two drafts. Grouped F32 operations made the mixed expert path practical. In a compiler-contended same-binary comparison, moving from 1,024 to 4,096 rows cut K2 prefill from 19.497 to 15.665 s and software expert reads from 232 to 87 GB. Wider chunks trade workspace and prefix-capture granularity for fewer expert rereads. [Native bring-up and chunk trials](2026-11-25-hy4-native-q3-k.md#20261125-hy4native-09--final-streamed-worker-and-prefill-chunk-size)

**Price dense projections and MLA separately.** On the 36 GB streamed control, 4K stage sampling attributed 4.71 s to routed experts, 3.36 s to dense attention projections, 2.03 s to MLA and 0.06 s to IndexPool and selection. The large GPU stages had room to improve. Stage sampling changes the execution schedule, so the rate decisions came from separate runs with profiling off. [HY4DENSE-01](2026-11-25-hy4-dense-prefill-expert-retention.md#20261125-hy4dense-01--split-the-prefill-attention-stage) · [Encoder-boundary control](2026-11-25-hy4-grouped-fragments.md#20261125-hy4frag-25--isolate-prefill-encoder-boundaries-from-sampling)

## Decode

**Streamed decode was often waiting for experts.** At a fixed 8,192-position load, one 28/40/48 GB arena sweep reached 32.29/42.76/43.83 tok/s warm K2 decode on the 4K fixture, with 43.62/24.27/18.41 GB of software reads. The 48 GB arm had only 0.923 GB of context budget left. In a separate fixed 36 GB bracket, repeat-demand retention raised second-request decode by 8–12% with no first-request gain. The 32K profile charged 2.287 s to demand waits spread across many expert banks. The three slowest banks accounted for about 13.5% of the cut time. [Arena sweep](2026-11-25-hy4-decode-cache-construction.md#20261125-hy4nextn-03--final-resident-build) · [Retention bracket](2026-11-25-hy4-dense-prefill-expert-retention.md#20261125-hy4dense-03--protect-repeated-decode-expert-demand) · [Per-bank wait attribution](2026-11-25-hy4-grouped-fragments.md#20261125-hy4frag-19--attribute-streamed-verifier-cuts-by-expert-bank)

**Prepared residency removed those read cuts when memory admitted it.** Initial mapped first demand stalled and took system swap to 65,461 MiB in the recorded sample. The successful `mapped-resident` path faulted in expert pages in 128 MiB intervals, watched pressure and swap, then requested GPU residency. The measured startup cost was 61–70 s. With both target and derived draft banks resident, separate Engram inputs let mapped verification submit one target command buffer per round. The combined path reduced that count from five. In a reversed same-binary test with Q8 row pairing and the contiguous IndexPool shortcut also enabled, warm K3 TypeScript went from 59.17 to 62.54 tok/s with the same outputs and 64 target rounds. The three changes were tested together. [Failed mapped control](2026-11-25-hy4-prefill-decode-profile.md) · [Prepared residency](2026-11-25-hy4-grouped-fragments.md#20261125-hy4frag-20--prepare-the-mapped-expert-banks-before-generation) · [Paired verifier comparison](2026-11-25-hy4-grouped-fragments.md#reverse-the-short-code-comparison)

**Speed up the draft work that remains after loading.** Resident Q8_0 embeddings reduced 16K catch-up to 0.207 s from 0.413–1.522 s in the surrounding controls. With both expert banks resident, batching five NextN cache projections reduced catch-up at 32K from 0.419 to 0.127 s and raised K2 decode from 41.60 to 44.32 tok/s in one pair with matching outputs and draft counts. The rebuilt packed path passed 4K, 16K, 32K and lifecycle checks. The 255.2 MB Q5_0 draft vocabulary, down from 394.4 MB for Q8_0, saved about 55 ms of draft work on warm short code in the first bracket. The qualified repeat also used two fewer target rounds. Keep Q8_0 as the worker default and Q5_0 in the two tuned profiles. [Embedding trial](2026-11-25-hy4-decode-cache-construction.md#20261125-hy4nextn-01--isolate-work-on-the-draft-and-target-paths) · [Packed cache qualification](2026-11-25-hy4-decode-cache-construction.md#qualify-the-packed-cache-in-the-rebuilt-worker) · [Draft vocabulary trial](2026-11-25-hy4-decode-cache-construction.md#20261126-hy4nextn-10--price-q5_0-for-the-draft-vocabulary)

**Choose drafts using useful tokens per second.** At 16K, K3 cut rounds from 80 to 68 and ran at 45.84–46.07 tok/s against 46.80–47.65 at K2. K1 was also slower, with a median paired ratio of 0.921 against K2 in an earlier bracket. In a separate 32K bracket, K3 cut rounds from 78 to 65, with 44.51 and 43.19 tok/s around a closing K2 at 43.33. Keep K2 recommended at long context. On a later short-code suite, K3 warm peaks were 67.82, 62.92 and 61.16 tok/s on TypeScript, Python and Rust, with all twelve serial and draft requests matching their fixture references. With a draft ceiling of seven, request-local lookup reached 60.61–61.18 tok/s in eight target rounds on repeated text. The ordinary coding fixtures offered zero lookup proposals, so lookup remains optional. ## Changes that lost or failed a gate

| Approach | Result and decision |

| Smaller or wider routed tiles | The F32 32-row tail only covered 21% of sampled tiles and showed no gain. Wider staging gave first-use results within drift. At 128 rows, there were 39% fewer tiles but 22% more routed GPU time, and first-use 4K prefill fell from 648–659 to 594–598 tok/s. Direct SIMD weight loads appeared faster before reversal and made the measured routed stage slower. Keep the 64-row fragment path. Requests may choose smaller windows within the fitted capacity. Both tuned profiles request compensated indexed sparse prefill. - **4K prefill.** The 711.61 tok/s run needs about 245 ms of prompt-phase savings to reach 743.20 on a 4,096-token fixture. Reprofile the whole prompt under fixed context and chunk settings before choosing another kernel.
